The seam, rendered as thread

The v0.5 seam was a flat dashed line, which is why it read as “broken lines”. These three render the thread as a lit cylinder with a shadow and needle holes. Left: actual size against a slice of rail and page. Right: the same at 4×. Sewn two thirds, open below, as on the site.

ATopstitch

Machine lockstitch, about 12 px stitch, 2 px gap. Each stitch dips into a needle hole at both ends. The quietest of the three; closest to what you see on a shirt placket.

Actual size

4× — where sewn meets open

BCorded

A continuous twisted cord laid in a shallow channel, like piping or a cording stitch. Reads as a rope of thread; most visible of the three. Where not yet laid, the empty channel and its holes show.

Actual size

4× — where sewn meets open

CSaddle stitch

Hand-sewn look: longer stitches, a heavier waxed thread, slight slant and irregularity, bigger holes. Warmer and more “atelier”; a touch less industrial than the factory is.

Actual size

4× — where sewn meets open

All three keep the v0.5 behaviour (sewn length follows the scroll, rips on page change, resews). Thread colour is the clay accent with a lit side and a shaded side; holes are small dents in the linen. The seam becomes 16 px wide (was 12) so the thread can have a body.
